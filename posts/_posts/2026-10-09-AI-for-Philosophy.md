I have recently published [a blog post at Daily Nous](https://dailynous.com/2026/10/08/ai-for-philosophy-progress-through-capital-intensive-philosophy-guest-post/) on how I think AI will transform philosophy. My post makes the case that AI will turn philosophy into a capital-intensive field, one where access to computational resources matters. Have a look, and feel free to leave some comments.

To support the blog post, I asked Claude to create an artifact illustrating some of its points, that is, to show how AI can support philosophy. To do so, the artifact presents the commitment-based theory of preference change that I have developed together with Michael Messerli in our [Cambridge Element _Preference Change_](https://www.cambridge.org/core/elements/preference-change/FB84E7A250C7262934BFC713E333223F).

The artifact provides tools to explore and test this theory. [It is publicly available](https://claude.ai/artifact/R7Tn3TQ6cduM4AeNBGc8cU). I had a look at it, but I can't guarantee all content is correct. If you encounter any issues, let me know!

The artifact can only partially illustrate my post because in the post I argued for the importance of scaling AI systems for philosophy. The resources used in creating the artifact were modest compared to what the post gestures at. Nevertheless, I hope it suggests some of the capacities that AI systems have now and how we might use them to make progress in philosophy.
